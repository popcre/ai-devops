LIKELY CAUSE, FOR WHOEVER FIXES IT
The raw output is full of ANSI escape sequences (ESC[0m, ESC[90m and similar) interleaved with the text, because the wrapper captures the interactive renderer's output rather than a plain-text stream. A verdict matcher applied to that stream will fail on any line where an escape sequence lands inside or adjacent to the matched token, or where the matcher anchors to start/end of line. The verdict line survived into the file intact, so the text is there; the detection over it is what failed.

Two things to check:
  1. Whether the matcher strips ANSI sequences before searching. A `sed 's/\x1b\[[0-9;]*m//g'` pass over the captured output made the verdict trivially greppable for me.
  2. Whether the matcher only inspects a bounded tail or a particular block, rather than the whole output. The verdict is the final line here, so a whole-output search would have found it.
